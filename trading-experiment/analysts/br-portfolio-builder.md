## Tax efficiency strategy (taxable account)

- **All holdings are equities/ETFs in a taxable cash account — no tax-loss harvesting has occurred to date** despite OMCL sitting on a genuine, sizable unrealized loss (−27.2%). At this book's scale (a ~$3.65 position), a harvest sale would generate a trivial capital loss (likely under $1.50) against essentially no realized gains elsewhere to offset, and OMCL is this book's highest-conviction remaining discount — selling to harvest and waiting out a 30-day wash-sale window would mean sitting out of the name precisely while its DCA gate is closest to firing. **Not recommended at current scale**, but flagged as a real deferred option once OMCL's tranche additions grow the position and/or a future year's realized gains create something worth offsetting.
- **VTI's 8/7 and 9/3 trims are the book's only realized activity to date** — both small, short-term (holding period well under a year), and taxed as ordinary income-rate short-term gains if net positive; given the trim amounts (~$2 and ~$2.50) the actual tax liability is de minimis, but the pattern is worth naming: every trim this book executes on a position held under a year realizes a short-term gain/loss, not a long-term one. As positions age past the one-year mark (VTI's oldest lot is now ~7 weeks old), future trims should preferentially sell the highest-cost-basis, longest-held lots (which Robinhood's FIFO default does not automatically optimize for) if a specific-lot-identification option is ever available — worth a standing ask to Jash about whether the Robinhood account supports specific-lot selection.
- **ETFs (VTI, VXUS, XLE) are inherently more tax-efficient than direct stock picks** for a taxable account (in-kind creation/redemption minimizes capital-gains distributions) — this book's core/satellite split already leans tax-efficient by construction, holding two ETFs at 54% of the pool.
- **No dividends have been a material factor yet** (book is 8 weeks old, all positions bought this summer) — but VTI/VXUS/XLE all pay qualified dividends; once dividend income becomes material, plan to reinvest via DRIP-equivalent fractional buys rather than let it sit as idle deployable cash, consistent with the DCA discipline below.
