## One-page investment policy statement

**Objective:** Compound a $50 base (plus accumulated trading profits) inside a ~$100 taxable cash account into a long-running, auditable track record, via a disciplined core/satellite equity and ETF portfolio. No options. Aggressive risk tolerance, short-to-medium horizon, long-run compounding ambition.

**Structure:**
- **Core (55% target):** VTI 30% (US broad market), VXUS 25% (ex-US diversification). The stabilizing base of the book, rebalanced only on ≥5pp drift or a genuine structural event, never on news-cycle noise.

- **Reserve (10% target):** deployable cash, held for the OMCL DCA gate and any future falsifiable, pre-committed entry trigger — never deployed reactively.

**Discipline:**
1. Every new position or add is governed by a pre-written, falsifiable, time-boxed trigger (rule 7) — never a same-minute reaction to news or price (rule 4).
2. Structural-break-only reaction rules govern every binary event (earnings, conferences, macro prints) — a bad number alone is never a sell signal; a cut to guidance, a lost contract, or a margin/bookings deterioration trend is (validated four times now: OMCL 7/30, NVDA 8/27, GEHC 9/10 — the plan's second live fire).
3. Every discretionary sizing decision requires cross-desk vetting (MS valuation + BW risk, rule 6) before capital moves — no single analyst's conviction is sufficient alone, and a cleared gate is not itself a buy signal absent an explicit overweight case (validated again today on GEHC).
4. Concentration triggers (NVDA single-name, NVDA+OMCL combined) are forced decision points, not automatic sells — but they are never allowed to lapse silently once flagged (rule 14).
5. Macro/rate-shock pauses (rule 6a) block new high-multiple adds mechanically, regardless of how compelling any single add looks in isolation, until the underlying data resolves — the 9/15-16 FOMC is the next real test.
6. Every time-box defaults to no-action at expiry (rule 16) — this book never forces a trade to meet a self-imposed deadline. Profit-linked gates (OMCL's DCA gate) are never substituted with price-linked ones just because a position keeps getting cheaper (rule 14's 9/9 decision, reaffirmed by today's evidence).
