- **The character of the chip risk changed overnight, not just its magnitude.** For three cycles this desk and others described this as a "sector rotation" or "rotation deepening." Trading halts on the Kospi are not rotation — they're panic-selling circuit breakers, a materially different risk regime. NVDA itself is barely down today (-0.27% live, $195.99 vs Monday's $196.51 close) despite the worst Asian chip session in over a year. Two honest readings, not one comfortable one: either (a) NVDA/GPU-logic names haven't caught down yet and this is a lagging risk, not an avoided one — memory names often lead compute names into a broader AI-capex repricing by a session or two — or (b) this is genuinely a memory-specific supply/competition shock (HBM/DRAM oversupply fears, a 2027 memory-price-peak call from brokers) that's mechanically distinct from NVDA's own GPU economics, and could even cut both ways for NVDA (cheaper HBM input costs vs. a broader signal that AI-hardware capex is cooling). I am not resolving this cleanly in either direction — both readings are live, and pretending otherwise would be false comfort.
- **VXUS is not the clean diversifier today that OMCL is.** VXUS fell -0.90% today, its sharpest move in over a week, because Samsung and SK Hynix are real VXUS constituents — this is the same look-through-concentration point BR flagged weeks ago, now visibly showing up in a single day's price action rather than as an abstract percentage.
