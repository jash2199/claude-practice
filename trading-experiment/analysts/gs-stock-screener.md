## Situation update: IONQ's reversal is the headline, a missile hits an ADNOC tanker in Hormuz, and Micron's chronic data-quality flag may finally be resolving

**IONQ (see #3 above) dominates this run.** This is a genuine, desk-embarrassing whipsaw worth naming directly: Friday's report was right that the DARPA news was real and that the market had already re-rated for it — but wrong to frame that re-rating as durable without stress-testing the underlying disclosure. Wolfpack's allegation, if substantiated, would mean the government-contract revenue this desk cited as "non-sentiment, government-validated" data was itself the weak link, not sentiment. This desk is not calling the allegations true — IonQ's denial is on record and short-seller reports have a mixed hit rate — but the honest read is that Friday's #2 ranking assumed a level of catalyst durability that a 48-hour later short report has already called into question. **Downgrading urgency, not deleting the name** — SkyWater integration and the Q2 beat/raise stand independent of the DARPA-specific allegation, and the September 8 Investor Day remains a real forward catalyst. But rule 6 (cross-vet before sizing) now carries extra weight here: MS/BW should not clear this gate on the old DARPA framing without addressing Wolfpack's claim on its merits.

**A missile hit an ADNOC tanker in the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend — an actual kinetic escalation, not just diplomatic friction.** This is a materially different category of news than the "theater diplomacy" rhetoric flagged 8/7: the UAE confirmed a vessel belonging to Abu Dhabi National Oil Company was targeted by a missile transiting Hormuz Saturday (no casualties reported). Layered on Iran's own confirmation that the Oman shipping-corridor arrangement, even once finalized, "will not by itself" reopen the strait pending unmet US conditions, and a fresh threat that non-US/Israel vessels could face a toll — this is the messiest the Hormuz picture has looked since this desk started tracking it. **This reinforces, more strongly than any prior update, the rule-3 geopolitical veto on OXY** — an active attack on a tanker is about as clean a "not yet resolved" signal as exists — and is consistent with state.md's own 8/10 note that XLE was the day's leader over the weekend read on a harder-line Iranian FM statement. GS and BW should both treat this as the clearest version yet of the standing thesis, not a new one.

**MU's chronic data-quality problem shows early signs of resolving, worth a cautious update not a clean bill of health.** Two independent fresh pulls this run converge on **~$877-880/share** (down modestly from a **$1,213.37 all-time closing high hit June 25**) — internally consistent with each other for the first time in weeks, unlike the $847-917 inconsistent range flagged 8/7 or the $180s-$200s figures referenced in earlier cycles. This desk is cautiously updating its stance: the current ~$880 level appears to reflect a real, large 2026 rally that this desk's own prior searches simply weren't capturing cleanly, not a persistent search-tool malfunction. **Still not sizing off any single read** (rule 4 discipline doesn't relax just because two numbers happened to agree once), but no longer flagging this as an active red flag — worth a dedicated MS DCF re-pull to confirm before this desk fully stands down the caveat.

**Market backdrop**: Friday (8/7) closed at record highs across all three major indices (Dow +0.3% to 54,036.93, S&P +0.6% to 7,757.64, Nasdaq +1.3% to 26,690.62) on the soft jobs report/dovish-rate narrative. Nothing in today's macro tape changes any conviction call below; flagging CPI Wednesday as the next scheduled macro catalyst this desk and JPM should both be tracking into the print.

**CRWD — a fresh insider-selling flag worth naming.** Beyond the already-known Threat Hunting Report/Fal.Con sentiment pop (+3.5-3.8% this week) and the XM Cyber IP acquisition, this run's search surfaces a pattern of Rule 144/Form 4 insider sales filings through late July and early August — enough volume that it's drawing institutional commentary about a potential local ceiling on valuation. Not a thesis-breaking data point on its own (insiders sell for many reasons), but combined with no MS DCF on file (rule 10 friction persists) and a stock already extended, this tips CRWD's risk/reward modestly worse than last report.

**AVGO, AMZN, PTCT — no material change beyond confirmation.** AVGO's Aug-4 close of $418.16 keeps it in "already extended, don't chase" territory per rule 2; GS's own removal of AVGO from the Conviction List (noted last report) stands, Morgan Stanley overweight reiterated.
