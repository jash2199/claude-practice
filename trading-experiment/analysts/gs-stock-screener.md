## Lead item — Strait of Hormuz re-escalated overnight; this changes the picture for OXY and XLE, not the verdict

**What happened:** US forces struck Iranian rocket launchers near the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday (8/30) after CENTCOM said it detected preparations for a fresh mining operation — the first direct US-Iran military exchange in roughly a month, ending what had been read (by this desk and others) as a genuine cooling-off period. An IRGC spokesman called it a "fatal mistake" and promised retaliation. Oil reacted immediately and materially: Brent (November) traded above $90/bbl (+~1.5% on the day, ~$89.46), WTI near $84.60-86. This directly reverses last week's incremental-but-fragile de-escalation narrative (the Iran-Oman shipping corridor, Trump's "open" claim contradicted by his own administration) — Iran's blockade-adjacent posture just got more aggressive, not less.

**Why this matters for this book specifically, not just headline color:**
1. **OXY (rule-3 veto): reinforced, not loosened.** Every open question this desk flagged Friday (Iran naming terms, Trump-vs-administration contradiction) is now overtaken by an actual weekend strike-and-retaliation-threat cycle — the opposite direction from resolution. No ambiguity here: the veto stands, and stands more firmly than it did Friday.
2. **XLE (held, basket-exempt): a live tailwind on the existing position, and a reason for MS to refresh its oil-adjusted DCF, not a reason to add.** XLE's own basket-exemption logic (its price already reflects the market's live view of the crisis) means today's spike should show up directly in the position's mark, likely favorably. But MS's standing verdict (-18.8% overvalued, do-not-add) was built on an oil assumption that a $90+ Brent print may already be stressing — this desk is flagging, not overriding: **MS should treat today's oil move as a trigger for a fresh composite re-read**, the same discipline this book applied on the way down in early August. Until that re-read lands, no change to the standing "hold, no add" stance from this desk.
3. **One inconsistency worth naming:** a separate WebSearch pull this run (an XLE-focused source) returned "WTI $80.93, down 2.8% today... OPEC slashed 2026 demand" — directly contradicting the multiply-sourced, explicitly-dated (8/31) Hormuz-strike reporting (Bloomberg, CNBC, Al Jazeera) showing oil rising. That figure reads as a stale or mis-dated cache, consistent with this book's standing rule-4 skepticism toward WebSearch pricing. **Weighting the multi-source, explicitly-dated strike coverage as the more reliable read; the trader's own live Robinhood/oil-proxy check should adjudicate before any oil-sensitive sizing decision.**
