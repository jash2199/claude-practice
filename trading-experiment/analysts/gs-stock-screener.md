## What changed since this morning's report — the relief rally reversed into a chip-specific selloff

This morning's read was written on a broad Iran-de-escalation relief rally (Dow/S&P/Nasdaq all up 1-1.5% at the open). That has substantially unwound over the last two hours — **not on any Hormuz/oil news reversing, but on a fresh, sector-specific semiconductor selloff**: SMH -3.7%, AMD and Teradyne both down 7%+, Micron -4-6% intraday, dragging NVDA down as much as -4.94% at 11:36 ET (partially recovered to -4.64% by 12:36 ET per state.md's live Robinhood read, $197.245/sh). The Dow/S&P are still holding modest gains on the oil relief; the Nasdaq has given most of its morning gain back. Two things worth being precise about:

1. **This is a sector rotation, not an NVDA-specific break.** The proximate NVDA story (the ~$250B OpenAI Ohio-datacenter financing-guarantee talks first reported yesterday) is unchanged information — what's new is that Bloomberg is now framing the total commitment closer to **~$750B** across financing + chip-purchase guarantees, and Jim Chanos went public this morning calling the arrangement "Nvidia financing roughly two-thirds of the cost of the chips it's selling to the project itself" — a sharper, more widely-quoted version of the circular-financing concern than yesterday's framing. This is incremental (a bigger number, a bigger name attached to the skepticism), not a new thesis. This desk's read: still a sentiment/positioning question about vendor-financing risk, not a demand, margin, or guidance problem — consistent with MS's own DCF gap (~26.8% overvalued) simply getting more attention today, not newly confirmed.
2. **MU, AVGO, ALAB, CRWD are all directly exposed to today's chip-sector rotation** — this list's #3, #7, #8, and (via AI-infrastructure sentiment) #6 names. None of them has a company-specific catalyst today; all four are getting caught in the same SMH-wide move as NVDA. Directionally bearish for anyone chasing today's dip, not a reason to abandon the underlying theses (MU's hard-pass DCF gate and CRWD's still-missing DCF are the standing constraints, unchanged by today's tape).

Separately, and more constructively: **MS delivered the fresh, oil-adjusted XLE DCF this desk and the trader both explicitly requested this morning.** Verdict: the overvaluation gap **narrowed to ~13.8%** (from ~19.3% on 7/24) — the Brent-normalization reference band analysts anchor to moved up roughly as much as spot moved down, so the "fast de-escalation would widen the overvaluation" bear case MS itself flagged on 7/24 did **not** materialize this time.
