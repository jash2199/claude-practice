## Situation update

**Market**: Broadly red again this afternoon, tech-led — the same combination flagged at midday (Hormuz escalation + long-end rate pressure) still driving the tape, with no relief this session. Bio-Rad Laboratories -32.4% (institutional selling, unrelated to this book's coverage) and Wolfspeed -7.6% ahead of its 8/19 print are today's standout non-coverage movers, underscoring how idiosyncratic single-day drops are running across the tape right now — a general caution flag for entry discipline on anything extended.

**MU — genuinely new negative development, worth escalating.** Netlist Inc. filed an ITC Section 337 complaint (plus a parallel Central District of California suit) against Micron, Supermicro, HPE, and Lenovo on August 12, alleging infringement of four patents (US 10,025,731; 10,217,523; 12,373,366; 12,675,407) reading on DDR5 RDIMM/MRDIMM memory products. Netlist is seeking exclusion and cease-and-desist orders — an import-ban remedy, not just damages — which is a materially different risk than a typical damages-only patent suit if it ever progresses that far (ITC cases move on an expedited track, commonly to trial within a year). MU is down >5% intraday off Monday's +4.13% high, giving back a chunk of the CXMT/HBM-driven momentum this desk flagged this morning. **This does not change this desk's rule-5 hard-pass stance** (already a pass on valuation grounds regardless), but it meaningfully weakens the "best-performing name on the sheet" framing this desk has been running the last two cycles — the moat case (CXMT has no real HBM presence) and the legal-risk case (a fresh ITC exclusion-order threat against a top-two DRAM supplier) are now both live and pulling in different directions. Demoting MU one notch in the ranking below to reflect this, even though the underlying rule-5 pass was never rank-dependent.

**AVGO — a second, distinct negative data point on top of the still-live VMware CVE.** BofA credit analysts sized Broadcom's exposure to the broader AI-capex circular-financing story directly for the first time: a ~$370B off-balance-sheet financing framework ("XPV platform") across the industry, with AVGO carrying up to $29B in lease backstops and potential residual-value guarantees if specialized AI hardware depreciates faster than expected. This is the same category of "circular financing" concern this book has been tracking on NVDA (the OpenAI/Ohio deal, now signed per MS's 8/18 read) — now showing up with a hard dollar figure on a second coverage name. AVGO -3.33% today, third red session in the last four when counting Friday's VMware-driven -5%. Demoting further; this is now two independent, company-specific negative developments stacked on a name this desk already wasn't pushing.

**OXY** — Barclays cut its price target to $71 from $75 today, the first negative-direction move this desk has seen on OXY after a string of raises this month (Wells Fargo to $79, Mizuho to $78, Truist to $63 from $57). Position stays fully rule-3 vetoed regardless — no change to Hormuz veto logic, if anything today's continued escalation (Iran's Ghalibaf reiterating no reopening without a lifted blockade, sanctions relief, and asset release) reinforces it.

No new diplomatic development found since this morning's read beyond the standing Trump/Oman friction and Iran's unchanged reopening conditions.
