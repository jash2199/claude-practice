## Headline: the relief rally is broadening into midday, oil keeps fading off this week's spike, and MU just picked up a genuinely more serious legal risk than the "legal fears" flag this desk logged yesterday

Three things move the needle since this morning's 09:44 ET report:

1. **The rally is broadening, not fading, into midday** — QQQ +1.19% intraday, tech leading, on the back of this morning's August CPI print. Worth a precision flag: the print itself was +0.4% MoM / **3.4% YoY**, which is a touch hotter than the "in-line" framing state.md's 09:36 ET run used — the market appears to be reading it as "not a hawkish surprise" rather than genuinely cool, consistent with the 10yr easing only 2bps to ~4.95% rather than reversing meaningfully. This is a market choosing to look through a still-elevated inflation print, not evidence the print itself improved. Rule 6a's pause stays exactly where it was.
2. **Oil continues fading off this week's spike** — Brent ~$106.1 (down from >$107-109 readings earlier this week), WTI ~$102.6-103.5, still historically elevated on the live Hormuz/Houthi-Saudi war premium but a second consecutive session of relief. Nothing new dated today on Hormuz/Bahrain beyond what this desk and BW already fully weighed this week (the Houthi strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities are a 9/8 story, already priced into both desks' escalation-tier read — not a fresh development, per rule 4).
3. **MU's legal overhang just got more serious, and this desk is correcting its own prior framing.** Yesterday's 09:44 ET report flagged MU purely as a *source-inconsistency* problem (three conflicting prices). Fresh WebSearch this run surfaces the actual substance underneath that price weakness: beyond the already-known Netlist ITC/patent actions (which could produce a US import-exclusion order on key DDR5 products), **a new US class-action lawsuit names Micron alongside Samsung and SK Hynix, alleging the three companies deliberately restricted DDR3/DDR4 production while shifting capacity toward higher-margin HBM** — a price-fixing allegation, not just a patent dispute. This is a materially different, and more serious, risk category than this desk had on file. Flagging loudly the run it's found, per rule 17.

Against that backdrop, PTCT delivered the one genuinely reassuring, name-specific data point of the morning at today's Cantor Fitzgerald healthcare conference (see #7 below) — directly responsive to the revenue-quality question this desk flagged as the driver of this week's sell-off.
