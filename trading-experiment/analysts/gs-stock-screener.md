## Headline: the macro backdrop got materially worse overnight even though this morning's tape is green — a direct hit on a US Fifth Fleet installation is the most serious escalation on file, and the 10yr is now on the cusp of 5%

Two macro developments since yesterday's 15:41 ET close deserve top billing, and they cut in different directions from this morning's price action:

1. **The 10-year Treasury is now reading ~4.95%, described by Bloomberg as "the cusp of 5%" and the highest level since 2007** — up 18bps just this week, driven by the Iran war pushing wholesale energy costs into August's hot PPI print, with today's CPI print (already in-line per state.md's 09:36 ET read) doing nothing to relieve it. This is a genuine deepening of rule 6a's rate-shock trigger, not a plateau — the pause on new NVDA/VTI/VXUS core-ups is, if anything, further from lifting than at any point since it fired 9/2, even as the 9/15-16 FOMC meeting draws closer as the next real resolution point.
2. **Iranian forces reportedly heavily damaged the US Fifth Fleet's base in Bahrain overnight, per the Acting US Navy Secretary — the first reported direct hit on a major US military installation in this conflict, not just tanker/vessel strikes.** This is a materially more serious escalation category than anything logged in prior reports (which have tracked tanker strikes, Jordan-base hits, and shipping-corridor disruption, not a hit degrading a forward-deployed US carrier group's home port). Oil's reaction is genuinely mixed this morning — some sources show Brent pulling back sharply (-1.4% to -3.6%) off this week's spike above $100-107, others show it still elevated near $105-107 — a dateline/source-consistency problem this desk flags rather than resolves (same pattern previously seen on OXY/FRO price strays); WTI is confirmed still >$100. Whatever the exact print, a strike on a US Navy base is a genuine tail-risk escalation that the market's muted, mixed reaction this morning has not yet fully priced.

Against that backdrop, state.md's 09:36 ET snapshot shows the trader's own book **all-green for the first time since 9/8**, +0.52% pool — a reminder that a single morning's relief rally (in-line CPI, a technical oil pullback) doesn't resolve either the rate trigger or the underlying military escalation.
