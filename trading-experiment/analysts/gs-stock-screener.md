Already held, already at/above BR's target weight — this remains a "thesis surviving contact with a bad tape" note, not a fresh add signal.
2. **SNDK and MU — the CXMT DRAM story gets sharper, not softer, and this desk is narrowing its distinction between the two names.** Fresh WebSearch this run confirms CXMT's Q2 DRAM share has already reached ~10% (up from ~4% a year ago), overtaking Micron in some readings of the ranking (Samsung 38%, SK Hynix 25%, Micron 24%, CXMT 10%) — a faster share gain than this morning's read implied, and CXMT is now also building out a **NAND flash R&D/production facility in Beijing**, a direct extension into SanDisk's core business, not just Micron's DRAM lane. **New and material: CXMT plans to begin producing HBM (high-bandwidth memory) starting late 2026** — this is the first dated confirmation this desk has seen that CXMT intends to compete directly in the AI-infra memory segment, not just mobile/mainstream DRAM. This doesn't flip either name's valuation call today (SNDK ~7x fwd, MU ~7.5x fwd vs. ~22x sector average, both still screening cheap on current numbers), but it meaningfully raises the multi-quarter competitive-erosion risk this desk flagged as "log it, don't ignore it" this morning — it should now be tracked as a live line item, not a watch-list footnote. **MU stays JPM's confirmed WAIT into the 9/30 print (6 days out); this desk reiterates: don't chase either name into that binary event.**
3. **New macro flag this run — Oracle's Project Jupiter force majeure notice is the first concrete crack in the AI-capex buildout story, and it belongs on this desk's watchlist alongside the rate shock, not as a stock pick.** Oracle sent a force-majeure notice on its $165B "Project Jupiter" data center (the Stargate-adjacent New Mexico buildout with OpenAI/SoftBank) after a key permit denial and debt financing trading below 90 cents on the dollar; Oracle stock fell ~4-5% on the news, Blue Owl Capital (the project's developer/landlord) fell sharply, while Bloom Energy — an alternative power supplier to data centers — has reportedly surged as capital looks for other ways into the AI-power trade. **This desk does not add Oracle, Blue Owl, or Bloom Energy to the screen this run** (none clear this book's valuation-first, $50-scale, fractional-share screening bar on a single headline), but flags this explicitly for BR/BW: it is the first dated, company-specific evidence that AI-datacenter financing and permitting friction is real, not hypothetical — worth weighing alongside the rate-shock/WACC-rebuild-clock discussion already running on this book, since a slower AI-capex buildout would eventually pressure NVDA's own demand assumptions (MS's DCF), not just Oracle's.

None of AVGO/AMD/INTC/ARM/MRVL/ALAB clear this book's rule-6 cross-vetting gate (MS DCF + BW risk read) this run — same as this morning, they remain watchlist-only, not pre-cleared for a same-run trigger the way GEHC is.
