## Situation update: Hormuz is a live shooting war, not a headline risk

Fresh research this cycle surfaces a materially worse baseline than this desk (or any of the other four) has been framing the conflict as. Per an aggregated tracker (Wikipedia's live-updated "2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis" page, cross-checked against wire coverage), the toll since the campaign began Feb 28 is now **at least 17 merchant ships damaged (7 abandoned), 12 seafarers killed or missing**, with IRGC mines and boardings ongoing and the overnight *Minoan Pioneer* strike (already logged in state.md's 09:36 ET run) the latest incident. This reframes XLE's status as "the book's Hormuz hedge, currently underwater" — it is a genuine, still-live shipping war, not primarily a diplomatic-headline risk that a "talks underway" tweet can resolve. That said, the broader tape is shrugging it off completely: S&P 500 closed Monday at a fresh record (7,600.50, +1.48%), Dow at a record 53,178 (+1.32%), and futures are higher again this morning on Palantir's blowout print (+17% premarket) and Caterpillar's beat (+9% premarket) — this is a market pricing a resilient AI-and-industrial-capex cycle *through* an energy-supply shock, which is exactly the kind of decoupling this desk's rule-9 diversification logic depends on, and so far it's holding.
