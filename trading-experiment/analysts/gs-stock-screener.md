## Detail

### 1. GEHC (GE HealthCare) — held, BUY / ADD candidate
- **P/E vs. sector**: ~24x forward vs. medtech peers in the mid-20s — roughly in line, not a screaming discount, but Needham's fresh 9/22 initiation (Buy, $93 PT) and the broader analyst tape (18 ratings, consensus Buy, avg. target $78.73) both sit well above today's $64.81.
- **Revenue growth**: low-single-digit organic (FY26 guide reaffirmed at the 9/9 Wells Fargo conference: 3-4% organic), but backlog +$2.6B YoY and book-to-bill 1.15x point to accelerating bookings ahead of revenue recognition.
- **Debt-to-equity**: moderate — BW's 10-Q-sourced net debt read (~$8.0B) is the one soft spot on this name; FCF conversion (Q2 $68M ex a one-time tariff refund) is still ramping toward the $1.6B FY26 guide.
- **Dividend**: modest (~0.9% yield), not the reason to own it.
- **Moat**: moderate — imaging/diagnostics incumbency and a large installed base, offset by GE-legacy margin drag.
- **Bull case (12mo)**: $85-90 (backlog converts, FCF ramp lands, multiple re-rates toward peer medtech).
- **Bear case (12mo)**: $58-60 (FCF conversion stalls, a genuine guidance cut).
- **Risk 3/10**: the lowest on this sheet — GS has carried this as its lowest-risk name for over a month, and nothing this run changes that.
- Note for the desk: price is currently *above* the book's own revised $62 GEHC revisit line (widened from $65 on 9/15) — no trigger live today, but worth flagging since this sheet is the fastest-refreshing of the five and the position has traded within a few points of that line repeatedly this month.

### 2. AVGO — BUY on weakness
- P/E has actually compressed — 45.0x trailing but only ~20.4x forward, and 9% *below* Broadcom's own 10-year average — a rare case of a mega-cap AI name getting cheaper as fundamentals improve. Revenue growth accelerated to 48.7% YoY with 48% operating margins and rising ROIC (30.9%). Custom AI-ASIC backlog (Google, Meta, and the newer hyperscaler wins) is the multi-year catalyst; fair-value estimates from sell-side have been marked *up*, not down, this month (to ~$692 by one shop, though the Street consensus PT of $515 is more conservative and worth treating as the base case). Pays a real dividend (~1.1%), unlike most of this list.

### 3. NVDA — held, BUY on dips
- Unchanged thesis from prior cycles: cheapest mega-cap AI multiple in the book (28.4x, 15% below the tech sector average), Data Center revenue still compounding at triple digits, and a balance sheet (0.17 D/E) that gives it more room to absorb a rate shock than any leveraged name on this sheet. This screen continues to flag a standing disagreement with MS's periodic "meaningfully overvalued" DCF reads on NVDA (see Disagreements) — the relative-multiple case here has been consistently cheaper than DCF-implied fair value for months, and the growth rate has kept validating the relative read over the DCF read so far.

### 4. AMD — SPECULATIVE BUY on a pullback, not a chase
- Fresh, genuinely new catalyst: the $8.2B all-stock acquisition of Fei-Fei Li's World Labs (announced 9/28, Li joining as EVP/Chief Scientist) is AMD's second-largest deal ever and a real strategic move into physical AI/robotics-simulation — not a buyback-and-guide headline. Stock is +3.6% on the news and up further intraday (traded as high as $629.75 today) — that is exactly the kind of one-day pop this desk's own entry discipline (mirroring the trading book's rule 2) says not to chase. Actionable only on a pullback into the $560-585 zone; the deal itself doesn't move AMD's FY26 numbers, only the multi-year physical-AI option value.

### 5. MU — HIGH CONVICTION, BUT DO NOT ENTER FRESH TODAY (earnings tomorrow after close)
- The valuation case is the best on this entire sheet by traditional screening metrics: 7.3x forward P/E against 48.9% trailing revenue growth and a 0.06 debt/equity ratio — genuinely cheap for a name riding the HBM/AI-memory supercycle, with Wall Street's average PT ($1,515) still above spot. But JPM's coverage (9/29 ~09:24 ET, freshest input on this name) has surfaced real, new, name-specific tail risk this week that changes the *sizing* conversation even if the valuation case is unchanged: two live ITC patent actions from Netlist, the newer one (instituted 9/23, filed 9/29) targeting Micron's HBM patents specifically and seeking exclusion orders against Micron *and* its downstream customers (Nvidia, Broadcom, Google) — i.e. a risk that could ripple into names #2 and #3 on this sheet too if it escalates — plus an increasingly confirmed Taiwan labor impasse (a specific ~83-month bonus ask, management calling it a closed door for this year). None of that shows up in a static P/E screen. **This screen's stance: MU is the highest-reward, highest-binary-risk name on the sheet — a name to watch through tomorrow's 4:30pm ET print, not to buy today.** Risk rating 8/10 reflects the binary event, not the underlying business.

### 6. MRVL — BUY, most reasonably-valued custom-silicon name on the sheet
- Consensus rating Buy/Strong Buy across multiple aggregators, average targets clustering $266-289 against today's $262.55 — modest but real upside with none of ARM's or SNDK's valuation extremity. Custom networking/AI-silicon exposure without the single-customer-concentration risk some peers carry.

### 7. SNDK — SPECULATIVE, do not chase; flag for BW
- The NAND supercycle story is real (52-week move from $112 to a $2,354 high, consensus PT still $2,137, 21 buys vs. 1 sell), but this is now the single most extended chart on the sheet — down ~27% from its own June high even as the target keeps rising. Lynx Research's fresh $2,450 target (above the all-time high) requires the supply thesis to hold with zero slippage. This screen is not comfortable ranking this higher than #7 until BW posts a risk read on it — it has not appeared in a BW report to date, which is itself worth flagging to the desk.

### 8. INTC — SPECULATIVE turnaround, momentum only
- Genuine operational green shoots (Foundry revenue +31% YoY, 18A/Panther Lake in high-volume production, potential 10% PC-chip price hikes) are real, but the foundry division is still losing ~$2.1B/quarter operationally and the stock has been whipsawing 20-30% within September alone ($115 → $140 territory → back to $115-124). This is a name for the risk-tolerant sleeve only, sized small.

### 9. ALAB — quality business, no cheap entry today
- Best-in-class connectivity/retimer franchise for AI racks, S&P 500 inclusion speculation still live, but richly valued and fell -3.65% on 9/28 (from $364.62 to $351.31) — a name to want, not a name to buy at today's print without a further pullback.

### 10. ARM — DEMOTED THIS CYCLE: flagging valuation risk, not recommending fresh entry
- 319.9x trailing P/E is genuinely disconnected from the 22.4% revenue growth underneath it, and the stock is already correcting (-7.92% on 9/28 alone, off a 9/25 high of $310.32) — sell-side commentary itself is now using the words "valuation risk grows." This screen carried ARM in the top 10 last cycle; this run it drops to #10 specifically because nothing about the multiple has been justified by this month's price action, and a rate-shock tape is exactly the environment that punishes 300x-P/E stories hardest. Not a sell for anyone already holding it — the licensing moat is real — but not a name this desk can recommend putting new capital into today.
