## Detail on the three actionable names

**MU (unheld).** The setup: 7.3x forward earnings on a business now compounding at +284-349% YoY, an oligopoly seat in HBM at the center of the AI capex cycle, and a fortress 0.06x D/E. The catch, per JPM's repeated flagging this week: MU has closed lower after 6 of its last 8 beats — a "great number, bad reaction" pattern this book has learned (via OMCL, twice) not to fight. Tonight's after-close print plus tomorrow's reaction is the actual decision point. Entry zone if the reaction is a clean "sell the news" dip with guidance intact: $900-950. Entry zone if the print is a genuine guide-up breakout: above $1,150 confirmed for two sessions. Stop-loss: ~12% below whichever entry fires, given the binary-event volatility already priced into the ~14% implied move.

**GEHC (held, $68.69 avg cost).** Unchanged thesis from the last several reports: MS's DCF ($71.16, gap ≈+10.8% undervalued, the widest read yet) plus a reaffirmed FY26 guide and improving backlog/book-to-bill make this the one name on the sheet trading below the desk's own fair-value estimate. The position is already near BR's ~4-5% pool target, so this is a "hold the line, don't chase a small dip into a bigger position" case rather than a fresh full-size add — consistent with rule 4 in state.md (a clean dip isn't automatically an add without an explicit overweight case).

**NVDA (held, $201.40 avg cost).** The bull case (cheapest forward multiple among AI megacaps, Data Center still accelerating) is real, but so is the bear case this desk has to own honestly: MS's own DCF reads NVDA at ~-10.6% overvalued on a spot basis, essentially flat at its worst-on-file reading. Both things are true because they're different lenses — GS's forward-multiple framing rewards the growth trajectory; MS's DCF penalizes today's price against a discounted cash-flow base case. Actionable-now is "hold as sized," not "add."

## Disagreements with other analysts' current outputs

- **NVDA valuation, GS vs. MS.** This desk's forward-multiple framing (cheapest megacap AI name, ~14.5x FY28) and MS's DCF framing (~-10.6% overvalued, essentially the worst reading on MS's entire sheet) point in opposite directions on the same stock. Neither desk is wrong on its own terms — a forward multiple and a spot DCF answer different questions — but readers of both reports should not average them into a false consensus. This desk's own view: the forward-multiple case is the more decision-relevant one for a name already held near its pool-target ceiling with clean concentration triggers, which is why "hold, no add" (the position followed anyway) is the right call even while disagreeing with MS's framing of the stock as expensive.
- **AI-complex sentiment overlay, GS vs. BW.** BW's latest risk grade keeps "look-through tech/AI concentration" red and flagged an unconfirmed ~$2T Anthropic IPO valuation figure as a fresh narrative risk for the whole AI-adjacent book. This desk's own independent WebSearch this run corroborates the Anthropic IPO chatter directionally (prior coverage centers on a ~$965B target, not $2T) but found nothing that changes the fundamental case for MU, NVDA, or the broader memory/AI-silicon complex screened above — sentiment-narrative risk is real and worth BW flagging, but it is not, on this desk's read, a fundamentals-level reason to downgrade any name on this sheet.
- **MU investability, GS vs. MS/BW gating.** This desk continues to rank MU #1 on pure screening merits (valuation, growth, balance sheet), while MS's standing DCF is a hard pass and BW's rule-6 risk gate on MU has never been opened. That gate exists for a reason this desk respects — MU is not investable for this book regardless of tonight's print — but the screening merits and the gating decision are two different questions, and this report answers the first one, not the second.
