## Situation update: IONQ's bear case deepens, Hormuz crosses further into blockade territory, PTCT's upgrade cluster completes

**IONQ — downgrading urgency further, not reversing course.** Friday's report flagged the DARPA-contract re-rating as durable; this morning's report flagged the Wolfpack short-seller allegation (undisclosed cancellation of Pentagon earmarks alleged to have funded up to 86% of 2022-24 revenue) as a direct attack on that thesis. This run adds a harder data point: **Morgan Stanley cut its stake in IonQ**, reported explicitly as adding fuel to the short-seller fire rather than counterbalancing it — a large institutional holder trimming into an active short campaign is a meaningfully different signal than retail-driven volatility. IonQ's denial ("false, misleading, and unsubstantiated claims... attempting to profit by driving down the price") remains on record, and the SkyWater integration and September 8 Investor Day are unaffected fundamentals. But between the allegation, the MS stake reduction, and the still-open secondary-resale overhang, this is now three independent negative data points inside four days on a name that was this desk's #2 conviction idea a week ago. **Recommend the risk rating move from 8 to 9** — one notch below MU's hard-pass level, reflecting a thesis now under compounding, not single-source, pressure.

**Hormuz has moved from "kinetic escalation" to something closer to an active blockade.** Beyond the ADNOC tanker missile strike already flagged this morning, fresh WebSearch this run finds **a second tanker hit August 8** — an Emirati vessel, confirmed by ADNOC and the UK's Maritime Trade Operations, plus a separate report of a tanker set on fire off the Omani coast. CENTCOM has now redirected 55 commercial vessels, disabled 2, and boarded 2 since the crisis intensified. Iran's Foreign Ministry restated Monday that the U.S. naval blockade must lift before Tehran will even discuss reopening the Strait. Oil confirms the deterioration: **WTI $80.42 (+2.9%), Brent $85.87 (+2.8%)** as of this run, both up further from this morning's already-elevated levels. **This is the strongest version yet of the rule-3 OXY veto** — an ongoing pattern of confirmed tanker strikes with an active naval interdiction underway is no longer a single-incident risk, it's a live conflict — and continues to validate XLE's position as a working hedge (day's leader again per the 12:36 ET check-in, +3.45%).

**Market backdrop turned modestly risk-off.** Nasdaq -0.43%, Dow -0.19%, S&P -0.12% intraday — a pullback from Friday's strongest week since April, with investors in wait-and-see mode ahead of Wednesday's CPI print. Nothing here changes any conviction call below, but it's the first red Monday session in a few weeks and worth noting as context for NVDA's -2.99% intraday move (per state.md, no company-specific catalyst found — reads as broad-tape softness plus profile-taking after last week's highs, not a name-specific issue).

**MU's data-quality flag is resolved, not just softening.** A clean fresh pull this run shows $877.57 (previous close $881.47, day range $847.02-$904.80) — fully consistent with the two independent reads this desk cited this morning (~$877-880) and no longer showing the internal inconsistency flagged in prior cycles. This desk is standing down the data-quality caveat entirely as of this report. The hard pass (rule 5, CXMT overhang) is unaffected by the price-data resolution.

**CRWD's insider-selling flag is more routine than last report implied — worth a partial walk-back.** Fresh detail this run: CEO George Kurtz's ~20,000-share sale (Aug 4-5, $204-$219 range) was executed under a **Rule 10b5-1 plan adopted back in January 2026** — a pre-scheduled, non-discretionary sale, not a reactive one. CFO Burt Podbere's ~7,622-share sale (Aug 3) was explicitly for **RSU tax withholding**, the most routine category of insider disposal that exists. Kurtz has still been a net seller of ~$96M over the trailing 12 months, so the pattern isn't nothing, but the specific early-August filings this desk flagged aren't the "informed insider dumping ahead of bad news" read the framing implied last report. Downgrading this from an active flag to background context.
