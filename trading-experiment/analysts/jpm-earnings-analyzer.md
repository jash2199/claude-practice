## INTC — Full Pre-Earnings Brief

**Estimated report: Thursday 2026-10-22, after market close** (Q3 FY2026). Status: **still company-unconfirmed** as of this run — every third-party calendar (Wall Street Horizon, MarketBeat, NextEarningsDate) labels it a projection based on last year's 10/23/2025 date and this year's cadence, not an Intel-issued date. Treat the 14-day window itself, not the date's certainty, as the trigger — if Intel pushes the date, this brief's urgency resets but its content mostly doesn't.

**Pattern: Intel has beaten its own guidance and consensus on revenue and non-GAAP EPS in all 4 of the last 4 quarters**, while GAAP results stayed deeply negative every quarter on restructuring/impairment/one-time charges (Mobileye goodwill twice, SCIP Ireland buy-in, escrowed-shares loss). The "beat-and-still-report-a-GAAP-loss" pattern is now the base case, not a surprise — watch whether Q3's charges roster is lighter (no obvious new one-time item has been flagged yet for Q3).

### Q3 2026 consensus / guidance for the upcoming print

- **Company guidance (issued 7/23/2026):** Revenue $15.8-16.8B; non-GAAP EPS $0.38; GAAP EPS $0.31; non-GAAP gross margin ~42%, GAAP ~41%.
- **Current street consensus (post-guidance, per Benzinga):** Revenue ~$16.40B; non-GAAP EPS ~$0.37, essentially in line with guidance (not materially sandbagged either direction, unlike the prior 4 quarters where consensus sat far below guidance).
- **Outlier:** Northland Securities reportedly at $0.30 EPS — below the guided range, worth noting as the one dissenting number found.
- **Framing:** This is the first quarter in the last 5 where consensus has converged close to guidance rather than sitting well below it. That changes the beat calculus — the "sandbagged-estimate, easy-beat" dynamic that powered the last 4 double-digit-gap beats is largely priced out this time. A repeat of a $0.15-0.20+ EPS beat would be a genuine surprise; a beat in the $0.02-0.05 range (normal-sized) would not carry the same reaction magnitude.

### Key metrics to watch this print

1. **Intel Foundry's operating loss trajectory and external customer revenue.** Q2 2026: Foundry revenue +31% to $5.8B, but a $2.1B operating loss, with outside-customer revenue still a small slice (~$293M cited by one bear-case source). The foundry loss narrowing (or not) quarter-over-quarter is the single most-watched line for the "is the turnaround real" debate.
2. **18A yield progress and Panther Lake ramp.** Management has cited 7-8%/month yield improvement (as of May 2026) and said Panther Lake entered mass production for a January 2026-ish launch; watch for a concrete external-customer commitment (reports cite Nvidia/AMD/OpenAI interest, none confirmed by Intel) — a named design win would be the single biggest re-rating catalyst in the quarter.
3. **Data Center & AI segment growth rate.** Has accelerated sharply: +22% (Q1) → +59% (Q2). Whether this holds, decelerates, or re-accelerates is the key read on whether Intel is genuinely capturing AI-server share or riding a one-quarter pull-forward.
4. **Client Computing trend.** Mixed recently (+13% Q2 after a -7% Q4 2025, +1% Q1) — watch for confirmation that PC demand (and the reported 10% CPU price hike effective 10/5/2026) is holding or squeezing margins.
5. **Gross margin cadence vs. the 42%/41% (non-GAAP/GAAP) guide** — margin trajectory is the market's proxy for whether the capex-heavy foundry buildout is becoming self-funding.
6. **Capex/free-cash-flow burn.** 2026 capex guided >$20B (raised from $18B), adjusted FCF was $(8.4)B in Q2 alone; any further raise to the capex number (with 2027 "significantly higher" already flagged) matters for the dilution/balance-sheet debate given the $20B stock offering priced in August.
7. **Any new one-time GAAP charge.** Every quarter this year has carried a large, mostly-unique impairment/restructuring item; Q3's absence or presence of a comparable item will swing the GAAP headline independent of the operating story.

### Management guidance from the last call (Q2 2026, 7/23/2026)

- Q3 2026 revenue $15.8-16.8B, non-GAAP EPS $0.38, GAAP EPS $0.31, non-GAAP gross margin ~42%.
- 2026 capex raised to >$20B (from $18B); 2027 capex flagged as "significantly higher," mostly US-based.
- Management said **industry-wide supply constraints are expected to persist into next year**, limiting Intel's ability to fully meet demand — a supply-side framing rather than a demand-concern framing, relevant context for any Q3 miss narrative.
- CFO language (echoed from Q3 2025 call forward) continues to frame current demand as outpacing supply.

**Pattern: 4 of the last 5 reports produced a double-digit-magnitude reaction (in absolute terms) or materially conflicting intraday/next-day moves.** This is one of the most binary setups on the GS screener, not a name where "small position, hold through" is a low-risk default.

### Bull case and price impact estimate

A clean beat-and-raise — Foundry loss narrowing further, a named external 18A/14A customer, Data Center & AI growth holding near 40-50%+ — would likely repeat the Q1-style double-digit pop (though less likely to replicate the full +23.6%, since the market is no longer as skeptically positioned as it was heading into that print; 32 of 48 analysts are at Hold today vs. 33-hold/9-buy in April, so less short-covering fuel). **Estimated bull-case impact: +10% to +20% next session**, with a credible foundry-customer announcement as the single catalyst that could push toward the upper end or beyond. 24/7 Wall St.'s most recent (9/30) bull-case model target is $137.37 (+19% from its $115.33 reference price, itself ~8 days stale as of this run).

### Bear case and downside risk estimate

Given consensus has converged close to guidance for the first time in 5 quarters, an in-line-or-light result (no acceleration in Data Center & AI, Foundry loss flat or wider, no customer news, a fresh unexplained GAAP charge) risks a re-rating lower simply because the easy-beat dynamic is gone — this is the quarter where "meeting guidance" may not be enough, unlike the prior 4. Combined with a stretched valuation (one source cites ~63x forward earnings at a $115 reference price), a disappointment has more room to fall than recent prints suggest. **Estimated bear-case impact: -10% to -20% next session**, roughly symmetric with the bull case given the binary setup; 24/7 Wall St.'s (9/30) bear-case model target is $99.28 (-14% from its reference price).

### Recommended play: buy before, sell before, or wait

**WAIT — not a position to initiate ahead of the print, and not a position this book currently holds, so no sell-before decision applies either.** Reasoning:
- No cost basis to protect or lock in gains on — this is purely a "would we open new risk" question, and the answer is no.
- The setup is genuinely binary (4 of 5 last reports moved double digits or produced conflicting intraday swings) with a real options-data gap (no fresher implied-move read than July) and a real price-staleness gap (no live quote newer than ~9/30, ~8 days old at best, with sources disagreeing by double-digit dollars even within that window).
- The easy-beat dynamic that powered the last 2 blowout reactions (Q1, and partially Q3 2025) appears to be fading — consensus has converged toward guidance for the first time, which cuts the probability of a repeat outsized pop without raising confidence in a repeat outsized drop.
- If this book wants exposure to the Q3 print specifically, the disciplined alternative is a small, pre-committed, falsifiable-trigger entry (per rule 7's pattern, used successfully for XLE/GEHC/OMCL) sized to the 9-11% expected move as a stop-distance reference — not a conviction buy. This desk is not recommending that trigger be set up given INTC has no existing cross-vet (no MS DCF, no BW risk read) on file, per rule 6's blocking logic that GS has applied to SNDK and MRVL.
- **Standing ask to MS/BW, consistent with this desk's catalyst-calendar role:** if INTC is to become more than a watch name before 10/22, it needs the same DCF/risk cross-vet SNDK is still waiting on — this desk flags the calendar urgency, not the investment case.
