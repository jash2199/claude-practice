## Decision summary

**MU is not held. Recommended play: WAIT — do not initiate a position ahead of tomorrow's 9/30 print, and if anything the case for caution strengthened overnight, not weakened.** Three sessions of pre-earnings selling against two above-Street price-target hikes is now a firmly established divergence, not a one-day quirk — the market is pricing something the sell-side notes aren't. HBM is the single most sentiment-sensitive, highest-multiple part of the bull case (Baird's target hike is explicitly built on "improved HBM margin outlook"); an exclusion-order risk on that specific product line, even a low-probability, multi-quarter-to-resolve one, is exactly the kind of tail risk that can turn an in-line print into a "the story just got more complicated" selloff, independent of the numbers themselves. That sits on top of, not instead of, the already-escalating Taiwan dispute (a specific 83-months-of-salary ask and a confirmed management "no room to adjust" impasse, board IPP decision landing the same week as the strike vote) and the standing options-implied ~8-11% move on a print with a genuinely bimodal five-quarter reaction history (three double-digit rallies, one -8%-to-20% post-beat selloff). None of this overrides MS's standing DCF hard-pass or BW's un-opened rule-6 risk gate — MU remains outside this book's investable set regardless of tomorrow's outcome. Net: this is now a name with *three* live, dated risk factors converging in the same 48-hour window (labor, litigation, and a binary print already priced for a clean beat-and-raise) rather than one. Watch through the print; do not chase either direction tomorrow or in the immediate aftermath without a fresh read.

## Key metrics to watch this print

1. **FY27 Q1 guidance (the real swing factor, not Q4 itself).** Watch for confirmation of low-teens sequential revenue growth and FY27 capacity being "locked in" — both Baird's and JPMorgan's theses hinge on forward guidance, not just Q4 clearing its own bar.
2. **Any direct management commentary on the two live ITC/Netlist actions (new this run).** With an HBM-specific exclusion-order case now filed (337-TA-1523) alongside the earlier DDR5 case, expect an analyst question on the call. A dismissive, confident answer ("standard industry patent litigation, no near-term operational risk") would defuse this quickly; silence or a hedged answer would keep it live as an overhang through the next quarter.
3. **Direct management commentary on the Taiwan labor dispute, now with a specific dollar ask on the table.** Watch whether management addresses the reported "no room to adjust this year's plan" impasse and the 83-months-of-salary gap directly, or continues to defer — an unresolved, open-ended answer here is a real near-term risk, not a background item, with both the strike vote and the board's IPP decision landing the same week.
4. **CXMT competitive positioning, ~10% DRAM share and rising.** Watch whether management reiterates that overlap remains concentrated in non-premium DRAM rather than HBM/server-DRAM — a confident, specific answer supports the bull case; a vague one does not.
5. **HBM4 ramp progress and yield/pricing commentary** for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform — still the single most sentiment-sensitive data point on the call, now with added relevance given the fresh HBM-specific patent action.
6. **FY27 capex guidance** — disciplined-expansion framing supports Baird's slower-supply-growth thesis; anything read as supply-glut risk 12-18 months out would not.
7. **Gross margin trajectory beyond the 86% Q4 guide** — today's above-Street target hikes are partly margin-driven; commentary falling short of that raised bar is a likely swing factor.
8. **China/export-control commentary** — standing sector risk, intertwined with the CXMT share-growth story.

## Bull case scenario and price impact estimate

Print beats guidance again (5-for-5), FY27 Q1 guide confirms low-teens sequential growth, HBM4 yield/pricing commentary is clean, margin commentary validates Baird's improved-HBM-margin thesis, management gives a confident and dated answer on the Taiwan IPP/strike-vote timeline, and — new bar this run — management credibly dismisses the ITC/Netlist HBM action as immaterial to near-term operations (standard industry licensing dispute, no near-term supply/customer risk). With two desks already at $1,520-1,540 targets, a genuine clean beat-and-raise-plus-reassurance quarter could see the Street average follow rather than lead for once. Precedent (Q1/Q3 FY26 reactions) suggests a **+10% to +16%** next-session move is plausible, though the stock would need to reclaim three sessions of pre-print weakness first just to get back to flat.

## Bear case scenario and downside risk estimate

Print merely meets the guided midpoint (no upside surprise), and/or FY27 Q1 guidance disappoints relative to the low-teens sequential bar — the Q2 FY26 precedent (beat but -8%, deepening to -20%) is the template, and three straight sessions of pre-print weakness despite bullish notes suggests the market may already be primed to read an in-line quarter as a disappointment. Downside is now compounded by two distinct new-since-yesterday factors, not one: (1) an evasive or non-committal answer on the Taiwan dispute, with the strike vote and board IPP decision landing the same week, against a reported management "no room to adjust" stance and an 83-months-of-salary gap between the two sides; and (2) any commentary that lends credibility to the fresh HBM-specific ITC/Netlist exclusion-order case, or a downstream customer (Nvidia/Broadcom/Google, all named in the filing) making noise about supply-chain risk. Plausible downside: **-8% to -20%**, with the added litigation and labor convergence arguably skewing toward the higher end of that range, or beyond it, versus prior reads.
