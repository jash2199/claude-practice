- Bar is already set high by the company's own guide, and today's second consecutive ~5% pre-earnings rally raises it further — a merely in-line print (hitting guidance exactly) is not obviously a "beat" in sentiment terms given the Q2 FY26 precedent above, and now has to clear an even richer starting valuation than yesterday's.

## Key metrics to watch this print

1. **FY27 Q1 guidance (the real swing factor, not Q4 itself).** Given Q2 FY26's precedent, the forward guide — not the trailing quarter's beat — is what will move the stock. Watch specifically for confirmation of the "low-teens sequential" FY27 Q1 revenue guide GS has modeled, and for any commentary on FY27 capacity being "locked in."
2. **HBM4 ramp progress and yield/pricing commentary.** Micron has begun volume production of HBM4 (36GB, 12-high) for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform — >2.8 TB/s bandwidth, >20% better power efficiency than HBM3E, ramping at ~2x the pace of HBM3E 12-high. Any deceleration or yield/pricing walkback here is the single most sentiment-sensitive data point on the call.
3. **FY27 capex guidance.** Management has already flagged construction-related capex up >$10B YoY in FY27 for HBM/DRAM capacity; watch whether that gets sized more precisely and whether it reads as disciplined expansion or a supply-glut risk being set up 12-18 months out.
4. **Gross margin trajectory beyond the 86% Q4 guide** — whether pricing discipline (multi-year DRAM/HBM agreements, tight supply) holds, or the print simply meets the guided midpoint with no upside surprise (which, per the Q2 precedent, may not be enough to rally a stock that has already run up hard into the print).
5. **Taiwan labor situation — now escalated, not just quantified.** The 9/21 Taoyuan mediation ended without agreement; management would not disclose its bonus counter-proposal pending board approval "early October" and signaled no room to move on this year's plan. The union now plans an actual **strike vote in early October** — a real procedural step, not just survey sentiment (an earlier internal poll had already shown ~80% support for a strike). Whether that vote lands before, during, or after the 9/30 print — and whether management addresses it directly on the call — is now a live, dated, binary-ish risk sitting right on top of the print itself, distinct from a Taichung-union mediation now confirmed for 10/22 (a separate, lower-urgency track).
6. **China/export-control commentary** — standing sector risk, watch for any incremental language.

## Management guidance from the last earnings call (Q3 FY26, 2026-06-24)

Guided Q4 FY26 revenue to $50B ± $1B, non-GAAP gross margin ~86%, non-GAAP EPS $31.00 ± $1.00 — all comfortably above where Q3's own guide had been set three months earlier ($33.5B rev / 81% GM / >$19 EPS, which the actual Q3 print then blew through). Management reiterated demand outstripping supply across HBM, DRAM, and NAND, expected to persist "well beyond calendar 2026," and disclosed over $100B in customer agreements alongside the print. This guide-and-beat-guide pattern for three consecutive quarters is precisely why Q2's post-beat selloff is the more instructive precedent than Q3's rally: the market has started pricing perfection into the guide itself, not just the trailing print.

## Bull case

Q4 comes in above the $50B/$31 guide (as every quarter has for a year) **and** management raises or reaffirms an aggressive FY27 Q1 sequential guide (GS's low-teens framework), with HBM4 yield/ramp commentary staying constructive **and** the Taiwan situation either produces a resolved/improved offer before or on the call, or the strike-vote process stalls without an actual work stoppage. Off today's higher **~$1,096** base, a repeat of the Q3-style reaction (+14-16%) would put MU in the **~$1,250-1,270** area; a more moderate beat-and-hold reaction in line with the trailing-four-print average (~+8-9%) would put it near **~$1,185-1,195**.

## Bear case

Q4 merely meets the guide (no beat) and/or FY27 Q1 guidance disappoints relative to the low-teens sequential bar the market has already built in — echoing the Q2 FY26 pattern where a genuine beat still produced an 8% same-day drop and a ~20% week-long slide as the market judged the *forward* guide insufficient. Layered on top: a failed mediation followed by an actual strike vote (and, in the tail case, a work stoppage at Micron's largest production base, representing >70% of its ~15,000-person Taiwan workforce) landing in or around print week would be a fresh, dated, operational overhang distinct from valuation fatigue — the first time this dispute has moved from "threat" to "no agreement reached, vote proceeding." At the options-implied ~10.3% move off the **~$1,096** base, downside would put MU in the **~$983-985** area; a repeat of the Q2-style ~20% week-long slide would put it closer to **~$875-880**. A confirmed strike, rather than just a vote, would be a fresh scenario this desk has not previously modeled and would need a same-run reassessment if it materializes.

## Recommended play

**WAIT.** MU is not held, so this is a "stay out" call rather than a position-management call. The setup remains a genuine two-sided one — four straight beats say the fundamentals are intact, but the two most recent guide-driven reactions (one big rally, one real selloff on a beat) say this stock's reaction function has become unpredictable around its own guidance, not just its trailing print. Today adds a second consecutive ~5% run-up (raising the bar further, with less room to be merely "good") on top of a labor dispute that just crossed from rhetoric into a failed mediation and a scheduled strike vote — two independent reasons not to hold this specific print, on top of (not a replacement for) MS's standing valuation veto and BW's un-opened rule-6 gate. If the team's other gates ever clear post-print, this desk would revisit with a fresh, lower-uncertainty entry point rather than sizing into the print itself. **Flagging for the next run: confirm whether the Taoyuan strike-vote date has been set for before or after 9/30 — that timing detail materially changes how this risk interacts with the print itself.**
