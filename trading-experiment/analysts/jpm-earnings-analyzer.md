## Brief 3: SNDK (Sandisk Corporation) — update

**Report date: fiscal Q1 2027, carried as Thursday, October 29, 2026. No primary-source re-confirmation found this run — flagging this explicitly rather than asserting false certainty; prior briefs have carried this date without a fresh company press-release citation, and this run's searches did not locate one either.** 10 days out on the carried date.

**What's changed since 10/16 — the most important update in this report: a sharp, multi-day, catalyst-driven sell-off, not just pricing-deceleration chatter.** SNDK has fallen roughly **18-20% over the past week** — one dated source puts it at $1,582 at the 10/9 close, sliding to ~$1,299-1,395 by 10/15-10/16 — and is down roughly 20% for October overall, now about 40% below its 6/22 all-time high of $2,354. The proximate catalyst named across multiple sources: **China's CXMT (a state-backed memory maker) announced plans to launch denser DDR5 server memory, built on a 4F² cell design, by year-end 2026** — the first time a sourced news item has pointed this specific competitive threat at server/NAND-adjacent memory economics rather than only at MU's DRAM franchise (where GS's screener has carried CXMT as a named risk for weeks). Compounding factors cited: reports of delayed Oracle data-center buildouts (a demand-side AI-capex worry) and broader market jitters over AI-related debt financing. One source also cites modest near-term spot-market NAND wafer-price softening, which — if it persists — would cut against the "NAND prices still rising, just decelerating" framing this desk and GS have both carried since early October (TrendForce's Q4 2026 +15-20% QoQ contract-price forecast). **Net: this is a real, multi-source-corroborated bear-case development, not noise — but it has not been independently verified against a live options chain or a fresh MS DCF rebuild, and price figures across sources disagree by as much as $100-200, so treat the magnitude as directionally right but not pinpoint-precise.**

**1. Last 4 quarters vs. estimates:** Fiscal Q4 2026 (reported ~August): adj. EPS $39.25 vs. revenue $8.965B — the company has raised guidance in 5 of its last 6 updates since May 2025, a strong beat-and-raise cadence. The August print itself drew a ~9% premarket drop despite being framed as "mixed" rather than a clean miss (Evercore cut its target to $2,800 from $3,100 on the same print) — another instance of this name selling off on guidance nuance even against a beat-heavy backdrop.

**2. Fiscal Q1 2027 consensus:** Company guidance (issued 8/5): revenue $10.3-10.8B, non-GAAP EPS $44-46 — both guided *above* the Street's prior consensus of ~$44.72 EPS / ~$11.16B revenue (note the revenue guide is actually below that stale consensus figure; the dispersion across sources here is wide and this desk could not fully reconcile it this run). In September, management reaffirmed the $10.3-10.8B revenue range.

**3. Key metrics to watch:** Gross margin against the 83-85% guide (fiscal Q4 ran at 84.6%) — the single number analysts are flagging as the real tell on whether elevated NAND pricing is holding; any commentary on the December-quarter NAND pricing trajectory; management's direct response to the CXMT server-memory threat (first earnings call where this specific competitive story will be addressable on the record); hyperscaler demand commentary given the Oracle-delay headlines.

**4. Segment breakdown:** Not independently built by this desk — SNDK's business is substantially NAND flash and SSD/storage products for AI/datacenter and enterprise customers, with a reported $42B in contracted multiyear supply agreements as of the last full build. No fresh segment-level estimate revisions found this run.

**5. Management guidance from last call (fiscal Q4, August):** Q1 FY27 revenue $10.3-10.8B, non-GAAP EPS $44-46 (both above prior Street consensus at the time); September reaffirmation of the revenue range.

**6. Options market implied move:** Two stale, conflicting figures found from the prior (April 2026) earnings cycle only — Benzinga Pro/Interactive Brokers cited ~16.57% implied move with the stock up ~317% YTD at the time; Invezz's independent read on the same cycle put it closer to ~21%, with implied volatility ~113-116%, and flagged that the market was pricing more downside protection than directional conviction. **Neither figure is current for the 10/29 event** — no live at-the-money straddle data for this cycle was retrievable. Given the stock's realized volatility over just the past week (~18-20% in six trading days), a double-digit implied move for the actual print would not be surprising, but this is this desk's inference, not a sourced number — flag accordingly.

**7. Historical stock price reaction after last 5 reports:** Consistently high-volatility; the most recent (fiscal Q4, August) produced a ~9% premarket drop on a "mixed" outlook despite continued analyst positivity on NAND strength and margins (several price-target cuts accompanied continued Outperform/Buy-equivalent ratings — a "good quarter, lower target" pattern similar to what GEHC and OMCL both also exhibit, suggesting elevated expectations are the common thread across all three names in this cycle's coverage).

**8. Bull case:** A clean beat on both lines with gross margin holding at or above the 85% high end of guide, explicit management pushback on the CXMT threat's near-term relevance (e.g., framing it as a 2027+ DRAM-adjacent story, not an immediate NAND-share risk), and reaffirmed or raised December-quarter pricing commentary. Given the stock's ~40% drawdown from its 6/22 high, a genuinely clean beat-and-raise could produce a sharp relief rally — plausibly +15% to +25% off the depressed base, mirroring the scale of moves this name has shown in both directions all year.

**9. Bear case:** Any gross-margin miss relative to the 83-85% guide, management acknowledging the CXMT threat as nearer-term relevant, or signs that the already-flagged spot-market NAND softening is spreading into contract pricing. Given the stock is already in an active, multi-week de-rating headed into the print (unusual — most of this book's covered names have been climbing or flat into their prints, not already falling), a bad print risks compounding an existing negative trend rather than reversing a rally. Price impact: -15% to -25%+, continuing the pattern of outsized single-day moves this name has shown all year.

**10. Recommended play: NO ENTRY — gated by valuation AND, now, a fresh sourced competitive-threat narrative; not a "buy the dip" situation.** MS's hard pass on SNDK (DCF-driven, bull-case ceiling well below recent trading levels) stands unchanged by this desk's own mandate — pricing continues to be the actual gate, not the print. The new CXMT/Oracle-delay news this week sharpens, rather than weakens, that gate: it is a genuine incremental bear-case data point that this desk is flagging explicitly for MS's next DCF rebuild (which should now weigh a NAND-adjacent competitive-threat scenario alongside the existing DRAM-focused CXMT assumption already built into MU's analysis), rather than something this desk is positioned to act on directly. This remains, and now more firmly, an unheld name under the standing rule-6 cross-vetting bar.
