1. **HBM4 ramp for NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform** — ramping ~2x the pace of HBM3E 12-high, yields ahead of expectations as of the Q3 call. Single most AI-capex-sensitive data point on the call.
2. **Data center revenue run-rate** — exceeded $25B in Q3 (>$100B annualized run-rate). Whether it holds, grows, or shows first signs of digestion.
3. **Segment gross margins** — Q3's 83-87% margins across all four BUs were unusually high/uniform; compression would signal pricing giving back some of the AI-driven spike.
4. **DRAM/NAND pricing trajectory** — the beat-and-raise story has been priced-side; any commentary on spot pricing rolling over matters more than a unit-volume miss.
5. **FY27 capex trajectory** — FY26 capex ~$27B net of incentives (~$10B in Q4 alone); FY27 quarterly capex guided above that. A number outrunning revenue growth expectations reads as confidence or overbuild risk depending on accompanying demand commentary.
6. **Tariff/trade commentary** — the Lutnick semiconductor tariff review (potentially extending to servers/laptops) still has not been addressed by MU management on any call. This print is the first opportunity to comment directly.

## Bull case (price base updated this run)

A clean beat-and-raise (Q4 actual above the $50B/$31 guide) plus a strong initial FY27 Q1 guide (the low-teens sequential growth GS is reportedly modeling) and reassuring HBM4/Vera Rubin yield commentary, with no incremental tariff damage, could plausibly repeat the Q3 FY26-style reaction: **+13-16%**. Off today's **~$978 base** (prior close), that puts the stock in the **~$1,105-1,135** area same-day — closer to the low end of the sell-side average PT ($1,513) than before, since the rally has already closed some of the gap. A bull scenario here is still "the next leg of the thesis gets confirmed," not "the thesis gets created," and is now running into a *more* extended entry point than 48 hours ago.

## Bear case (price base updated this run)

Given no recent miss to calibrate from, the more probable bear path remains Q2 FY26's pattern: a beat that fails to clear an already-fully-priced bar — now compounded by the stock having rallied ~5% in the two sessions immediately before the print, on top of the still-unaddressed Lutnick tariff review and the AI-capex-digestion narrative that produced MU's own -5.2% single-session move on 9/14 (sentiment only, no company news). A guide that merely meets rather than beats FY27 Q1 expectations, or any margin-compression/tariff commentary, could plausibly produce a **-8% to -16%** move. Off today's **~$978 base**, that's a **~$820-900** area — meaning the pre-print rally itself is now part of the downside math: a "meets, doesn't beat" outcome has more room to give back than it did when the stock was sitting at $925-945 earlier this week.
