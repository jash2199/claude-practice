## Decision summary

**MU is not held. Recommended play: WAIT — do not initiate a position ahead of the 9/30 print.** Call is unchanged, but the setup has de-risked slightly rather than deteriorated further. The stock closed Thursday at $1,071.88 (recovering from an intraday $1,055-1,072 low) and is quoted this morning around **$1,080.53**, back near its pre-pullback range and comfortably above Monday's $1,015.80 close — the one-day dip on Thursday looks like it found support, not the start of a valuation-fatigue unwind. On CXMT: fresh reporting today gives a materially more precise picture than Thursday's headline-level alarm. CXMT's newest 24-gigabit LPDDR5X mass-production milestone is aimed at **mobile-centric and established DRAM segments**, not the premium server-DRAM and HBM segments that actually drive Micron's AI-cycle bull case — Micron's own reporting still shows it holding 23.3% of global DRAM revenue (Q2 2026) versus CXMT's 9.5% (up from 4% a year ago), and the competitive overlap where it matters most for this stock (HBM4 for Vera Rubin) remains limited for now. This is a genuine structural watch item long-term (and CXMT is separately reported to be preparing a NAND push too — a new, later-stage risk for both MU and SNDK), but it is not the near-term print risk yesterday's framing implied. On labor: Micron already announced record Taiwan bonuses (35-68 months' pay, 9/11) ahead of the board's Incentive Pay Plan approval, which the union and company both still expect in October — this reads as the company getting ahead of the dispute rather than stonewalling into the print, a modest positive shift from the more adversarial framing carried in this desk's reports through mid-September. The specific Oct 9 board date cited in prior reports could not be re-verified today; treat the board decision as "expected in October, timing imprecise" rather than a confirmed post-earnings landmine. Options are still pricing a **~10-11% implied move** for the print (10.28% per the latest Barchart-style read on the 10/2 expiry, consistent with yesterday), well above MU's trailing four-print average realized move of ~8.1%. None of this overrides MS's standing DCF hard-pass or BW's un-opened rule-6 risk gate. Net: this remains a binary, high-variance event this desk continues to recommend not chasing — the risk picture is marginally cleaner than 24 hours ago, not materially different.

## Key metrics to watch this print

1. **FY27 Q1 guidance (the real swing factor, not Q4 itself).** Watch for confirmation of the "low-teens sequential" FY27 Q1 revenue guide GS has modeled, and any commentary on FY27 capacity being "locked in."
2. **Competitive response to CXMT, now with a segment-specificity question.** Management will almost certainly get a direct question about CXMT. Watch for whether management explicitly confirms today's read (CXMT's push is mobile/established-segment-focused, limited HBM/server-DRAM overlap) or pushes back / hedges on it — that distinction is now the actual data point, not just "does management dismiss CXMT."
3. **HBM4 ramp progress and yield/pricing commentary.** Micron has begun volume production of HBM4 (36GB, 12-high) for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform — >2.8 TB/s bandwidth, ramping at ~2x the pace of HBM3E 12-high. This remains the single most sentiment-sensitive data point on the call, and is the segment CXMT has not yet reached.
4. **FY27 capex guidance.** Management has flagged construction-related capex up >$10B YoY in FY27 for HBM/DRAM capacity; watch whether that reads as disciplined expansion or a supply-glut risk being set up 12-18 months out.
5. **Gross margin trajectory beyond the 86% Q4 guide** — whether pricing discipline holds, or the print simply meets the guided midpoint with no upside surprise (which, per the Q2 precedent, may not be enough to rally the stock even after today's recovery).
6. **Taiwan labor / Incentive Pay Plan timing.** The board's formal IPP approval is still expected in October, timing now unconfirmed rather than pinned to a specific date; watch whether management addresses the 9/11 bonus announcement and union response directly on the call, and whether a strike vote (previously flagged as "early October," union-side) lands before or after the print.
7. **China/export-control commentary** — standing sector risk, now intertwined with the CXMT competitive question and CXMT's reported NAND-push ambitions (a SNDK-relevant angle too).
