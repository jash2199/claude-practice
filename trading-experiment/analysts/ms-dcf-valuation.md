## 2. Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE) — full rebuild, Brent input finally refreshed

### Why this is a rebuild, not a price-roll
XLE's $58.9/sh fair value and $76/bbl Brent assumption have stood unchanged since 7/27 — now the single most overdue input on this book, flagged in eight consecutive reports. Today's WebSearch broke that wall: Reuters-sourced coverage (via Business Recorder/Tribune syndication) and independent corroboration (Kucoin/CNBC/HDFC Sky/Discovery Alert) all place **Brent at ~$104.4-105/bbl through 10/14-10/15/2026**, driven by escalating Strait of Hormuz tanker attacks (nine tankers attacked in the past week per one report; Hormuz flows estimated ~30% below pre-crisis levels) only partially offset by a US move to ease shipping sanctions and higher Russian/Middle-East output elsewhere. The EIA's October outlook raised its **Q4 2026 Brent forecast by $14 to ~$105/bbl average** on the same disruption. This is a genuine, dated, well-corroborated break in the staleness this desk has been flagging since 7/27 — large enough, and important enough to XLE specifically, to warrant a real rebuild rather than another carryforward note.

### Methodology and transparency caveat
XLE is a 24-holding ETF (XOM ~22-24%, CVX ~16-17%, COP ~7%, remainder spread across E&P, midstream, and equipment/services names), not a single company — this desk's standing $58.9/sh fair value was built bottom-up against that composite at a flat $76/bbl assumption, but the year-by-year constituent-level FCF build itself predates this persona file's retained git history (the file lineage here only goes back to 10/9; the original 7/27 build lived in an earlier version of this book not preserved in this repo). Rather than discard the standing model, this desk rebuilt it as a **top-down scaling exercise**: holding the standing WACC/terminal-growth framework and PV-split structure constant, and re-deriving year-by-year fair value off a revised Brent price deck against an assumed aggregate sector cash breakeven of **~$45/bbl** (consistent with Chevron's own disclosed <$50 Brent dividend-and-capex breakeven, scaled modestly for the ETF's broader E&P/services mix). This is a defensible approximation, not a from-scratch constituent-level build — flagged plainly as this run's key methodology caveat.

### The price deck — explicitly NOT extrapolating today's crisis price for 5 years
A VP-level build does not take a geopolitical-disruption spot price and hold it flat for a 5-year DCF horizon. Futures-curve logic (backwardation typical of supply-shock-driven spikes) and this run's own research — CNBC's own framing this cycle that "oil's up 9.5% but Exxon and Chevron barely moved, and that's the point" — both argue equity markets are already treating this move with real skepticism about its persistence. This desk built a **reverting base-case price deck**: elevated near-term, decaying back to the standing $76/bbl long-run assumption by year 5 (i.e., no change at all to the book's own long-run equilibrium view — only the near-term path is new).

| **Base case Brent ($/bbl)** | 95 | 85 | 80 | 78 | 76 |
| Implied margin/bbl (less $45 breakeven) | 50 | 40 | 35 | 33 | 31 |

Applying these ratios to the standing model's explicit-period PV (≈35% of the old $58.9 FV, ≈$20.6/sh, scaled by the simple 5-year average ratio of 1.219x ≈ $25.1/sh) and leaving the terminal-value PV (≈65% of FV, ≈$38.3/sh) **unchanged**, since by year 5 the deck has fully reverted to the book's existing $76/bbl long-run view:

**Rebuilt fair value ≈ $25.1 + $38.3 ≈ $63.4/sh** (WACC held at ~11.0%, g at 3.0%, both effectively unchanged given the Rf move's immateriality noted above).

## Key assumptions that could break these models

**XLE-specific (new this run — the highest-risk build on the book):**
- **The price-deck reversion path is the single biggest judgment call in this entire report.** This desk assumed the Hormuz disruption fades over ~3-4 years back to the standing $76/bbl long-run view. If the disruption resolves within months (bear case: fair value falls to $50.9, re-widening the overvaluation gap past its prior worst-ever reading) or proves structurally persistent for years (bull case: fair value rises to $71.8, flipping XLE to undervalued), the verdict itself changes — this is not a name where "overvalued, hold" is a low-conviction call to make right now.
- **The $45/bbl aggregate breakeven assumption is this desk's own estimate**, anchored to Chevron's disclosed <$50 breakeven but not independently verified across all 24 of XLE's constituents (XOM has not disclosed a comparable figure in sourced results this run).
- **The scaling methodology itself is an approximation** of a standing model whose full constituent-level build predates this persona file's retained history — a from-scratch, bottom-up rebuild (sourcing each major constituent's own production/opex guidance) would be more rigorous and should be the next priority the next time this desk has bandwidth for a full model refresh rather than a scaling exercise.
- **Geopolitical resolution timing is inherently unmodelable with precision** — any confirmed Hormuz de-escalation, a Strait reopening to normal flow, or conversely a further escalation (e.g., a confirmed closure rather than reduced flow) would each warrant an immediate re-run of this sensitivity table rather than waiting for the next scheduled cycle.
