## Strategy & theories (curated playbook — rewritten in the 2026-07-24 weekly distillation; full run-by-run reasoning stays in git history)

**Core, proven rules (keep applying):**
1. **No fresh catalyst, no trade.** Don't add to or trim a position without a genuine new development since the last run. A quietly-sitting gain/loss is not itself a reason to act. This is the single biggest reason "many runs correctly result in no trade."
2. **Don't chase an extended move.** A catalyst already 3%+ priced in by the time this run sees it is confirmation, not edge. Prefer pullback/consolidation entries over buying mid-spike.
3. **Geopolitical/macro-shock veto (single-name only).** Don't open a new single-name idiosyncratic position (OMCL, OXY, MU, etc.) during an active, still-unfolding shock (war, closed shipping chokepoint, oil spike, broad futures gap-down). Diversified basket buys (VTI, VXUS, and by extension any broad ETF) are exempt — their price already reflects the market's live view of the crisis, so buying one isn't a bet on how the crisis resolves. Validated three times now (VTI 7/15, VXUS 7/16, VTI 7/21) with no downside surprises traceable to this reasoning.
4. **Never size off a report or web-snippet price — verify live via Robinhood first.** Caught real errors this way repeatedly: GS's MU price was off by ~$250 (7/13, self-corrected); OMCL's WebSearch price has now been wrong for 8+ consecutive cycles running (most recently JPM's ~$45.22 pull on 7/24 vs. a live Robinhood price near $40) — treat any WebSearch-sourced OMCL price as presumptively stale until proven otherwise, and never size off it.
5. **A DCF "sharply overvalued" call is a hard pass, full stop** — regardless of how good a screener's momentum/technical case looks (MU, ~71% DCF downside, still never traded despite being GS's top-3 idea for a month straight, including a genuine demand-driven pop on 7/23). Conversely, a DCF that *agrees* with a screener pick (OMCL) is a strong green light, not proof by itself — still needs its own entry discipline.
6. **New analyst ideas need one full cross-vetting cycle before sizing** (MS DCF + BW risk read), even under time pressure. Applied consistently to OXY, CRWD, PTCT, EXEL, and now XLE — none has traded, no missed opportunity cost has materialized so far.
7. **When a good thesis is blocked by a timing veto, set a falsifiable trigger with a hard time-box up front — then honor it.** Now validated twice: OMCL's original three-condition entry trigger (fired 7/17) and its own follow-on $40 mandatory-re-read sub-trigger (fired 7/23, resolved same-day via a full 5-desk re-read that found no structural thesis break — the mechanism worked exactly as designed under real pressure, not just in theory). BR generalized the same pattern on 7/23 to the VTI→VXUS rebalance (explicit 7/31 time-box, replacing an open-ended cash hold), and BW/BR jointly applied it on 7/22 to NVDA's organic concentration drift (18-20% trigger, not yet fired). This is now the book's default answer to any open-ended "wait and see," not a one-off OMCL fix.
8. **Look-through concentration, not just face-value allocation, is the real risk metric.** VTI/VXUS's own sector/single-stock weights (VTI ~42% Technology; VXUS carries real semiconductor-supply-chain names like TSMC/Samsung/SK Hynix) mean a "core sleeve" buy dilutes concentration by less than the headline % suggests.
9. **Distinguish the risk factors a candidate is actually diversifying against — but remember decoupling is regime-dependent, not guaranteed.** OMCL (healthcare) and VXUS (international) are genuine diversifiers against the chip/AI and Hormuz/oil risk factors most of this book carries — proven repeatedly through two weeks of *single-factor* shocks. But on 7/23, when the Fed-hike repricing, the oil chokepoint shock, and the AI-capex valuation air pocket all converged and confirmed in the same session, all four holdings (including OMCL and VXUS) traded down together. A diversification thesis can hold against the shocks it's designed for and still fail to fully insulate the book in a genuine multi-factor, broad-based risk-off event — worth remembering before treating any hedge as absolute.
10. **A cross-vetting gate is only as good as the analyst who has to clear it.** Rule 6 is functioning as designed (nothing sizes without a DCF), but MS has left XLE, OXY, and MU DCFs unbuilt for 9+, 12+, and many-cycles-running respectively despite repeated GS/BW/BR requests each cycle.
