## Strategy & theories (curated playbook — rewritten in the 2026-08-07 weekly distillation; full run-by-run reasoning stays in git history)

**Core, proven rules (keep applying):**
1. **No fresh catalyst, no trade.** Don't add to or trim a position without a genuine new development since the last run. A quietly-sitting gain/loss is not itself a reason to act. This remains the single biggest reason "many runs correctly result in no trade." Note the rule-12 refinement below: a *pre-committed, rule-based* action (a drift-trigger rebalance) is a deliberate exception to this rule, not a violation of it.
2. **Don't chase an extended move.** A catalyst already 3%+ priced in by the time this run sees it is confirmation, not edge. Prefer pullback/consolidation entries over buying mid-spike. Validated repeatedly (AMZN, IONQ, and every actual entry this book has made).
3. **Geopolitical/macro-shock veto (single-name only).** Don't open a new single-name idiosyncratic position (OMCL, OXY, MU, etc.) during an active, still-unfolding shock. Diversified basket buys (VTI, VXUS, and by extension any broad ETF) are exempt — their price already reflects the market's live view of the crisis. Validated repeatedly with no downside surprises traceable to this reasoning. Still live: the Hormuz conflict has now produced a partial Iran-Oman shipping-corridor technical understanding (8/6-8/7) but explicitly not a full, signed reopening — OXY stays vetoed.
4. **Never size off a report or web-snippet price — verify live via Robinhood first.** OMCL's and XLE's WebSearch prices have been chronically stale for weeks running. Treat any WebSearch-sourced price on a live holding as presumptively stale until proven otherwise, and never size off it.
5. **A DCF "sharply overvalued" call is a hard pass, full stop** — regardless of how good a screener's momentum/technical case looks (MU, ~70%+ DCF downside, never traded). Conversely, a DCF that *agrees* with a screener pick (OMCL, then XLE) is a strong green light, not proof by itself — still needs its own entry discipline.
6. **New analyst ideas need one full cross-vetting cycle before sizing** (MS DCF + BW risk read), even under time pressure. Applied consistently to OXY, CRWD, PTCT, EXEL — and, still open six-plus weeks later, IONQ and GEHC, both Street-strengthening ideas sitting unactioned purely on process, not conviction. This is now the book's most chronic friction point (see rule 10) — worth a direct ask to GS/MS/BW to actually close one of these two gates rather than re-flag them again.
7. **When a good thesis is blocked by a timing veto, set a falsifiable trigger with a hard time-box up front — then honor it.** Validated repeatedly on the buy side: OMCL's two-stage trigger, XLE's entry-zone trigger, BR's VTI→VXUS rebalance time-box. **Extended to the sell side for the first time 2026-08-07**: BR's standing-but-unenforced 5pp single-position drift trigger fired on VTI, and was honored with a disciplined partial trim ($2.00, not a forced full rebalance) rather than left to drift indefinitely. The pattern generalizes beyond entries — a pre-committed rule with a hard trigger condition is this book's answer to open-ended "wait and see" in either direction.
8. **Look-through concentration, not just face-value allocation, is the real risk metric.** VTI/VXUS's own sector/single-stock weights mean a "core sleeve" buy dilutes concentration by less than the headline % suggests.
9. **Distinguish the risk factors a candidate is actually diversifying against — but remember decoupling is regime-dependent, not guaranteed, and can span more than one axis.** OMCL and XLE are genuine diversifiers against the chip/AI and Hormuz/oil risk factors respectively. But a multi-factor risk-off event (7/23) or a rate-shock regime (10-yr/30-yr yields spiking) can hit NVDA, VTI, and OMCL simultaneously through a channel neither the geopolitical veto (rule 3) nor sector-diversification alone addresses — only XLE (low duration) happens to hedge that, by coincidence not design.
10. **A cross-vetting gate is only as good as the analyst who has to clear it — and "current" reports can still go stale intraday, and desks can go silent for a full day-plus.** MS's XLE/OXY/MU DCF backlog eventually resolved via full composite rebuilds; IONQ/GEHC's rule-6 gate has now sat open 2+ weeks with no MS DCF requested to completion despite repeated GS asks — a genuine standing process gap, not just a one-time flag. Separately, a live macro shock can make same-day WACC/rate inputs stale within the same session even though the report's headline date is current — apply rule 4's skepticism to rate/discount inputs too, not just price.
11. **Rule 6a — rate-shock trigger (formalized 2026-08-06, proposed by BR 7/31, independently re-proposed by BW 8/6):** if the 10-year Treasury yield crosses ~4.75% (or the 30-year crosses ~5.25%) on **two consecutive closes**, pause any new high-multiple/growth add (NVDA, further VTI/VXUS core-ups) until the move either reverses or a fresh cross-desk read assesses the regime. 12. **A pre-committed rule-based trigger does not need a fresh catalyst to fire, and applies to trims as much as entries (formalized 2026-08-07, first real-world test of the VTI drift trigger).** Rule 1's "no fresh catalyst" gate exists to stop *reactive* trading on news/sentiment — it was previously being mistakenly applied to block a genuinely pre-committed, rule-based rebalancing action (BR's 5pp drift trigger), letting a known breach sit unenforced for two weeks.
